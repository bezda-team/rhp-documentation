Each tower is a single `Bar`, and its length is the height in metres.
What makes it a building rather than a bar is the roofline, which the Bar wears as a **shape**:

```js
const gable = shape(["M", 0, 0], ["L", "-26px", 0], ["L", 1, .5], ["L", "-26px", 1], ["L", 0, 1], ["Z"]);
```

The first coordinate of each point runs along the value axis, where 0 is the tower's base, 1 is its roof, and `"-26px"` is 26px in from the roof.
The second runs across the tower.
That mix is the point: the crown stays 26px however tall the data makes the tower, while the tower's width is still its own.

rhp turns the shape for a vertical chart and for a bar that runs backward, so each roofline is written once and the skyline survives being laid on its side.

The gallery version adds:

- **Towers that overlap, so the skyline has no gaps.**
  `thick` is a share of the slat's band, and here it comes from the data and goes **above 1**, so each tower spills into its neighbours' bands.
  With `inset: 0` as well, thirteen towers meet edge to edge and read as one silhouette rather than thirteen bars.
- **Eight rooflines**, all in [shapes.js](https://github.com/bezda-team/rhp-documentation): an art deco crown that steps in on the diagonal, a tiered spire, a gothic pinnacle, a gable, chamfered corners, walls that lean in, a dome drawn with one curve, and a crown that flares on two.
- **A thin bar for the spire**, from the roof to the tip, in the one colour that isn't the silhouette.
- **The name turned on its side** with `writing-mode`, so thirteen names fit under thirteen towers.
- **A note hung at the top of the spire** on a `Place`, which the slat's CSS fades in on hover.
