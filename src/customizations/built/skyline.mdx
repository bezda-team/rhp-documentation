A tower is several bars stacked on top of each other.
The first runs from 0 to the mass of the building.
Each setback above it is another `Bar`, starting where the last one ended and narrower than it, which is what gives the tower its crown.
Then a thin `Bar` from the top of the crown to `d.tip` is the spire.

Because a Bar can start at any value, none of this needs a stacking helper: each block simply begins where the one below it stopped.

The gallery version adds:

- **Towers that overlap, so the skyline has no gaps.**
  `thick` is a share of the slat's band, and here it comes from the data and goes **above 1**, so each tower spills into its neighbours' bands.
  With `inset: 0` as well, the thirteen towers meet edge to edge and read as one silhouette instead of thirteen bars.
- **Crowns that keep their proportions.**
  A setback is a share of its own tower's width, not a fixed number of pixels, so a wide tower gets a wide crown and the shapes survive new data.
- **The name turned on its side.**
  `writing-mode: vertical-rl` in the slat's CSS, under `:vertical`, so thirteen names fit under thirteen towers, and the chart laid on its side goes back to ordinary labels.
- **A note hung at the top of the spire.**
  A `Place` at `d.tip` holds the text that says how much of the tower is spire, and the slat's CSS fades it in on hover.
