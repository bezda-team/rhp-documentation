Two plots in one chart, where picking in one changes the other.

**One `Chart`, two `Plot`s.**
A Chart draws its Plots over each other, so the two share its box and each decides what to do with it:

```jsx
<Chart scale={[0, 100]} ticks={false} style={{ "--pitch": "10.5cqw", "--bars": "calc(var(--pitch) * 4)" }}>
  <Plot overlap …>{PartSlat}</Plot>
  <Plot keyboard style={{ "justify-self": "end" }} …>{GenSlat}</Plot>
</Chart>
```

rhp pins a Plot's own width and height, so the tubes are sized by their slats instead.
`thickness` is the room one slat gets, and given as a CSS length in `cqw` it is a share of the chart's width, so the tubes and the pie keep their proportions at any size.
The pie's CSS draws in what the tubes leave.
On a narrow poster a container query puts the tubes under the pie instead, at the whole width.

**The pie says more than the tubes.**
A tube is how many of a generation use AI to write.
The pie splits those by how often, and keeps the rest:

```js
const split = (using, daily) => [daily, using - daily, 100 - using];
```

With nothing picked, the pie is everyone: each generation's split, weighed by how many people are in it.

**Picking lives in the example.**
rhp keeps no selection, so a signal holds the picked generation and both Plots read it.
A Plot's props are its data, so a click handler can't be passed in as one.
Instead the slat marks itself with `data-gen`, and the poster listens and finds it with `closest()`.
`keyboard` on the `Plot` adds Tab and the arrow keys, and Enter or Space picks.
A click anywhere else on the poster, or Escape, goes back to everyone.

**The pie is the gallery's pie**: edges pushed aside so the gap is one width, a stroke in the wedge's own color for the round corners, and `animated` moving the two ends of each span so the rim stays round as it changes.
Each wedge's svg casts a short drop shadow, so the wedges sit just off the page.
A wedge too narrow for its edges to reach the hole comes to a point where they cross, rather than bending them in to the hole, so the gap stays even beside the smallest wedge.

**A small part's label goes to a corner.**
The pie is drawn at .86 of a square, so the square's corners have room for a label.
Under a sixth of the pie a label has no room inside, so it goes to a corner, and the corners are shared out so the labels are as near their wedges as they can all be.
A dotted line runs from the label straight across or straight down to the nearest part of its wedge it can reach that way.
Failing that, it runs out to the square's edge and along it, so it never crosses the pie.
Each label measures itself, so its line stops at its edge, and it moves to its place on the same clock as its wedge.
The line fades in once the label has arrived, and then follows the wedge.

**The tubes take the height the paragraph leaves.**
The paragraph over them wraps to more lines on a narrow poster, so its height is measured and given to the Chart as `--side`.
The slat's CSS gives the fill and the outline a track of their own, from under the paragraph down to the labels, in place of rhp's whole height:

```css
.slat:vertical :is(.fill, .tube) {
  bottom: calc(var(--low) + var(--rhp-lo) * var(--tall));
  height: calc((var(--rhp-hi) - var(--rhp-lo)) * var(--tall));
}
```

Each tube is filled in a color of its own, checked to stay apart from its neighbours for colorblind readers too, and named under it so the color is never the only way to tell.
The fill and the outline are drawn by an `<i>` inside each `Bar`, because rhp turns transitions off on its own blocks in the JS version and picking is not a change of data.

**Flat**, the pie is one stacked bar at the top and the tubes lie down under it.
