A slat is made of blocks.
You give a block numbers from your data, and rhp works out where on the chart those numbers fall.
You can put as many blocks in a slat as you like, and any elements of your own around or inside them.
For many scatter points, [ManyDots](/reference/manydots/) draws a collection directly inside a Chart.

## Bar

A bar from `from` to `to`.
`from` is 0 if you leave it out.
`thick` sets how thick the bar is: a length like `"10px"`, or a fraction of the slat's thickness, like `0.5` for half of it.

<Demo name="block-bar" height={150} />

A Bar whose `to` is below its `from` runs backward, like a negative value.

## Dot

A round point at `at`.
`size` sets its diameter (10px by default).

<Demo name="block-dot" height={170} />

## ManyDots

A bulk collection of plain HTML points, positioned by `at` and `cross` on the Chart's two scales.
Use ManyDots directly inside the Chart, without a Plot or a slat per point.
Choose it explicitly for a large scatter collection; Dot remains available at any point count for individual blocks and custom slat content.

Points can share styling, use category classes, or have individual colors, sizes, shapes, and styles.
The collection uses light DOM, so document queries and native event delegation can reach its points.

See the [ManyDots page](/reference/manydots/) for the live example, complete props, interaction patterns, and current source-build availability.

## Tick

A short line across the slat at `at`: a goal, a median, a threshold.

<Demo name="block-tick" height={150} />

## Label

Text on the chart.

- `at={value}` puts it just past that value. Add `side="before"` to put it just before instead, which suits negative bars.
- `edge="start"` puts it before the chart, where names go. `edge="end"` puts it after.

<Demo name="block-label" height={170} />

## Cell

A cell that fills the space it is given, colored by its `value`: the chart's scale runs from the theme's `low` color to its `high` one.
Cells make heatmaps.
Here each day's slat holds a small Plot of its own, one cell per hour (more about this in [charts inside slats](/guides/nesting/)).

<Demo name="block-cell" height={140} />

## Place

A Place draws nothing at all: no size, no colour, nothing on the screen.
It only marks a spot, so whatever you put inside it sits at `at` on the scale.
Use it for the pieces that are yours to draw, a badge, a bubble, a photo, a needle, where rhp only has to say where they go.

<Demo name="blocks-place" height={230} />

- `across` puts it across the band, from 0 to 1, and `cross` puts it on a [second axis](/guides/scales/#a-second-axis) instead.
- Everything inside it is yours: rhp adds no padding, no background and no text rules to undo.
- Without it you would borrow another block and switch its appearance off, which is CSS you would have to keep in step with rhp.

## Area

A smooth shape over the scale, from a list of `points`, each `[value, height]`.
The tallest point reaches the full thickness of the slat.
Add `mirror` to draw it both ways from the middle, for violin plots.
Add `smooth` to join the points with curves instead of straight lines.

<Demo name="block-area" height={150} />

For a shape made from raw samples, the `density` helper computes the points for you.

## Line

A line through a list of `points`, each `[value, y]`.
In a slat it is a sparkline.
The largest y reaches the top of the slat, so each slat is scaled on its own.
Set `peak` to the y that should reach the top, and then the slats can be compared with each other.
Add `fill` to fill under the line, and `smooth` to join the points with curves.

<Demo name="block-line" height={170} />

On a chart with a [second axis](/guides/scales/#a-second-axis), y is on that axis instead: a line chart.

## What every block takes

Every block also takes what an HTML element takes:

- `class` and `style` to style it,
- `color`: a theme color (`"series-2"`, `"positive"`) or any CSS color,
- event handlers such as `onClick`, and attributes such as `title`, `data-*` and `aria-*`,
- children: text, an image, or any other element, drawn inside the block.

Bar, Dot, Tick, Label and Cell also take `shape`, an outline to draw instead of their rectangle.
`shape()` makes one from SVG's path commands, in the block's own box:

```jsx
import { shape } from "@bezda/rhp";

// a bar with a pointed end: 26px back from its end, it narrows to a point at the end's middle
const arrow = shape(["M", 0, 0], ["L", "-26px", 0], ["L", 1, 0.5], ["L", "-26px", 1], ["L", 0, 1], ["Z"]);

<Bar to={d.value} shape={arrow} />
```

The first number of each point runs along the scale, from the block's start (0) to its end (1), and the second across the band, from 0 to 1.
A length such as `"26px"` is measured from the start, and `"-26px"` from the end.
rhp turns the shape for a vertical chart and for a bar that runs backward, so you write it once.

The full list of props is in the [blocks reference](/reference/blocks/).
